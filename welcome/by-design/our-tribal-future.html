The big idea

Belonging is an instinct

Samson calls it the Tribe Drive: an evolved urge to belong to groups that helped our ancestors survive. We can't remove it, he argues, but we can choose where it goes, into small, real, trusting groups, or into abstract camps that feed division.1,4,5

Three lessons

1

The trust paradox

Our minds were built for kin and friends, yet we cooperate with millions of strangers. Shared symbols and rituals make that trust possible.4

2

We're under-ritualized

Modern life has lost many of the rituals that once bound groups together.4

3

Beliefs as badges

Samson argues that some beliefs work as signals of loyalty to a group, which is why facts alone rarely change minds.4,6

The framework

The Tribe Drive, redirected4,5

Samson's argument in four steps. Some of his proposals are debated by other scholars.4,6

1

The Tribe Drive

Belonging is basic

We evolved to belong to nested groups, from family to band to tribe.1,6

Try: Notice which groups you say “we” about.
2

Campcrafting

Small groups, close together

Invest in face-to-face groups like the hunter-gatherer camp of 20 to 30 people.4,6

Try: Start with a weekly meal.4
3

Ritual

The currency of trust

Rituals and symbols help strangers trust each other.4

Try: Give a group a name or an opening ritual.
4

Metabelief

A vaccine for tribalism

Beliefs that update with evidence, toward a shared identity he calls Team Human.4,5

Try: Ask what evidence would change your mind.

From the book

Tools to try

Map your tribes

Inspired by the author's interview on The Hub.5

  1. List the groups you're proud to belong to.
  2. Mark each as face-to-face or abstract.
  3. Move time and energy toward the face-to-face ones.

Campcraft a circle

Applying the book's campcrafting and ritual ideas.4

  1. Invite 4 to 10 people to a recurring gathering.
  2. Give it a name or a simple ritual.
  3. Hold it for eight weeks, then decide together whether to continue.

Make it yours

Try it eight weeks

  1. Map your tribes.
  2. Choose one face-to-face group to invest in.
  3. Add one ritual to it.
  4. Practise metabelief: write down a belief and what would change your mind.
