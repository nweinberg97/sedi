The big idea

Redesign before you resign

Most people assume the only fix for a frustrating job is a new one. Burnett and Evans argue that you can almost always make your current job work better first, by reframing stuck beliefs, running small experiments and treating “good enough for now” as a base to build on.1,4,5

Three lessons

1

Reframe stuck beliefs

Every chapter starts with a dysfunctional belief and a reframe. Feeling like a cog in a machine becomes being a lever that can move it.4

2

Good enough for now

Not forever, just for now. Accepting that gives you a stable base to improve from, instead of waiting to arrive.4

3

Overwhelm isn't burnout

Overwhelm can be designed around. True burnout or a toxic workplace may be a reason to leave.5,7

The framework

Don't Resign, Redesign3,7

Four options to try, smallest first, before deciding to quit.7

1

Reframe and re-enlist

A new why for the same job

Find a fresh reason the work matters to you.7

Try: Write down what this job gives you that you'd miss.
2

Remodel

Change the role

Cosmetic or structural changes to what you do.7

Try: Trade one task you dread for one you'd enjoy.
3

Relocate

Move within the company

Find a different role where you are.7

Try: Have coffee with a team you admire.
4

Reinvent

Retrain inside

Build new skills toward a new career without leaving.7

Try: Take on a stretch project.

From the book

Tools to try

The Good Work Journal

This book's version of the Good Time Journal.4

  1. Each day, note what you learned, what you initiated, and who you helped.
  2. Keep it going for three to four weeks.
  3. Each week, look for surprises and which category dominates.
  4. Plan one way to grow the missing category.

The Maker's Mix

Instead of money versus meaning, rate three kinds of making.3,5

  1. Score money-making, impact-making and expression-making from 1 to 100.
  2. Set your ideal mix, like faders on a mixing desk.
  3. Close the biggest gap with a small prototype, inside or outside work.
