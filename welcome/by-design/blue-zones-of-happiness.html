The big idea

Shape your surroundings

Buettner's reporting suggests the happiest people don't try harder. They live in places that make good days easy: walkable streets, short commutes, close friends nearby, homes that invite rest. Change your environment, and your behavior follows.1,4,5

Three lessons

1

Social time matters

Buettner points to research suggesting the happiest people spend about six hours a day with others, far more than most people do.6,8

2

The commute is a tax

Long commutes drag down happiness. Living close enough to walk to work is ideal.4,6

3

Purpose beats pay

In Denmark, a strong safety net frees people to choose work for interest rather than need.3,5

The framework

The Three Strands of Happiness1,3

Buettner braids happiness from three strands, and finds a country that excels at each.1,3

1

Pleasure

Enjoying your days · Costa Rica

Day-to-day positive emotion. Costa Ricans enjoy strong family and faith ties and lots of daily socializing.3,4

Try: Build more small, shared rituals into each day.
2

Purpose

Meaning and engagement · Denmark

Meaningful activity that uses your strengths, supported by trust, clubs and volunteering.3,4,5

Try: Join a club or volunteer.6
3

Pride

Satisfaction with life · Singapore

How you rate your life overall. Singapore scores high thanks to security and clear paths to success.3,4

Try: Live within your means and set reachable goals.7

From the book

Tools to try

The Happiness Test

The book's self-test for finding your weakest strand.1,4

  1. Take the test, or rate pleasure, purpose and pride from 1 to 10.
  2. Find your weakest strand.
  3. Read the chapter for the area of life most tied to it.

Design your Life Radius

The book's practical chapters, one area of life at a time.1,2

  1. Community: favour walkability, nature and trust.4
  2. Workplace and home: shorten the commute; keep the TV out of the main room.4
  3. Social life: a few close friends, a weekly family meal.6
  4. Inner life: know your purpose, volunteer, try new things.9
