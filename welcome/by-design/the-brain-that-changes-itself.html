The big idea

What you practise, you become

For a long time, scientists thought each brain region did one fixed job. Doidge tells the story of the research that showed otherwise: what we repeatedly do and pay attention to physically reshapes the brain. That cuts both ways, so choose what you rehearse.1,3,4

Three lessons

1

Use it or lose it

Brain territory is contested. Skills you practise claim more of it; skills you neglect lose ground.4,5

2

The plastic paradox

The flexibility that lets us change also locks in rigid habits. Doidge compares it to ski tracks: the more a path is used, the harder it is to leave.6,7

3

Attention is the key

Doidge reports Michael Merzenich's finding that lasting change in brain maps needs close attention. Divided attention doesn't produce it.8

The framework

How the brain rewires3,4

Four ideas that run through the book's case studies.3,4

1

Fire together, wire together

Repetition builds connections

Neurons active at the same time strengthen their links. The phrase sums up Donald Hebb's rule.10,12

Try: Pair a habit with a consistent cue and place.
2

Competitive plasticity

Skills compete for space

Practised skills expand; neglected ones shrink.4,5

Try: Practise what you want to keep.
3

Mental practice

Imagining changes the brain

Alvaro Pascual-Leone found mental practice of piano changed brain maps much like physical practice.8

Try: Rehearse vividly before you practise.
4

Constraint

Block the easy way

Edward Taub's therapy restrains the strong limb so the weak one relearns, in small graded steps.9

Try: Remove the shortcut you lean on.

From the book

Tools to try

Mental rehearsal

Inspired by Pascual-Leone's research in the book.8

  1. Pick one skill.
  2. Rehearse it vividly in your head for five minutes.
  3. Then practise it for real with full attention.
  4. Track progress over a week.

Interrupt and refocus

From the book's chapter on obsessive loops.5

  1. When a worry loop or urge starts, name it as a habit circuit, not a fact.
  2. Switch to a planned, rewarding activity for about 15 minutes.
  3. Log it, and notice the loop weakening over time.

Make it yours

Try it this month

  1. Pick one skill to grow.
  2. Rehearse it in your head for five minutes a day.
  3. Practise for ten minutes with full attention and one shortcut removed.
  4. Keep it little and often: steady practice beats cramming.11
