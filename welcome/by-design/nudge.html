The big idea

There is no neutral design

Someone always decides how options are arranged: what comes first, what's the default, what takes effort. Thaler and Sunstein call that person a choice architect. Since design always influences choice, they argue for designing on purpose to help people while keeping every option open, which they call libertarian paternalism.2,4

Three lessons

1

Humans, not Econs

Economic models assume perfectly rational Econs. Real Humans think with a fast Automatic System and a slow Reflective System, and make predictable mistakes.2,7

2

Defaults are powerful

Many people stick with whatever happens if they do nothing. Automatic enrolment in retirement savings is the book's classic example.2,8

3

Beware of sludge

The Final Edition adds the opposite of a nudge: friction that makes good choices harder, like long forms and hard-to-cancel subscriptions.5,6

The framework

NUDGES9

The authors' checklist of choice-architecture tools. Each letter is one lever.9

1

iNcentives

Make costs visible

People respond to incentives they actually notice.9

Try: Show the real cost at the moment of choice.
2

Understand mappings

Connect choices to outcomes

Help people see what each option means for things they care about.9

Try: Translate features into results.
3

Defaults

What happens if you do nothing

Set the default to what most people would choose on reflection.2,9

Try: Make the good option automatic.
4

Give feedback · Expect error

Design for real people

Tell people how they're doing, and design so common mistakes are hard to make, like a cash machine that returns your card first.9

Try: Add a forcing function for the usual slip.
5

Structure complex choices

Simplify when options multiply

As choices grow, help people narrow them down.9

Try: Offer filters for what matters most.

From the book

Tools to try

Save More Tomorrow

A program by Thaler and Shlomo Benartzi, described in the book.2,10

  1. Commit now to save more in the future.
  2. Tie each increase to your next pay rise.
  3. Let it happen automatically, and stay free to opt out.

A sludge audit

Applying the Final Edition's sludge chapter to your own life.5,6

  1. Pick a good habit you keep skipping.
  2. Time each step it takes.
  3. Remove or prefill one step.
  4. Add one step of friction to a habit you want less of.

Make it yours

Try it this week

  1. List five recurring choices: savings, subscriptions, your phone's home screen, snacks, calendar invites.
  2. For each, write what happens if you do nothing.
  3. Change two defaults to what you'd choose on reflection.
  4. Run a sludge audit on one good habit.

Reflect

Where are you living with a default you never chose?

Whose choices do you shape, at home or at work?
