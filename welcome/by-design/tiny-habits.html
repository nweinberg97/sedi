The big idea

Start tiny, and make it feel good

Fogg argues that relying on motivation is a trap, because it rises and falls like a wave. Shrink a new habit until it's easy on your worst day, attach it to something you already do, and celebrate the moment you do it. The good feeling is what wires the habit in.1,4,5

Three lessons

1

Motivation is unreliable

Use a high-motivation moment for one-off actions, like signing up or setting things up. For daily habits, rely on ease instead.4

2

Emotions create habits

Fogg's term for the feeling of success right after a behavior is Shine. A quick, genuine celebration does more to build a habit than repetition alone.5

3

Help yourself feel successful

Choose behaviors you actually want to do and can do, rather than ones you feel you should. Small wins build the confidence for bigger ones.1,6

The framework

The Fogg Behavior Model3

B = MAP

A Behavior happens when Motivation, Ability and a Prompt come together at the same moment. If a behavior isn't happening, one of the three is missing.3

1

Motivation

Your desire to do it

Real, but it rises and falls through the day in what Fogg calls the Motivation Wave.4

Try: Match yourself with behaviors you already want.1
2

Ability

How easy it is

Fogg's Ability Chain: time, money, physical effort, mental effort and fit with your routine. The weakest link decides how hard it feels.6

Try: Make it tiny: a Starter Step, or a scaled-back version.6
3

Prompt

What tells you to do it now

Three kinds: Person prompts (a feeling), Context prompts (something around you) and Action prompts (a routine you already do).7

Try: Use an Action prompt, which Fogg calls an Anchor.7

From the book

Tools to try

The ABC recipe

Fogg's formula for a new habit: Anchor, Behavior, Celebration.8,11

  1. Pick an Anchor: a routine you never skip, like starting the coffee machine.
  2. Choose a tiny Behavior that takes under 30 seconds.
  3. Write it as: After I [anchor], I will [behavior].
  4. Celebrate right away, in a way that feels real to you.

Swarm and Focus Map

Fogg's way to pick the right behaviors for a goal.6,10

  1. Write the outcome you want at the centre of a page.
  2. Swarm it: list every specific behavior that could help.
  3. Plot each one by impact and by how likely you are to do it.
  4. Start with the Golden Behaviors: high on both.

Make it yours

Try it this week

  1. Write one Tiny Habits recipe using the ABC formula.
  2. Try Fogg's Maui Habit: each morning, as your feet touch the floor, tell yourself it's going to be a great day.9
  3. Turn an annoyance into a cue, which Fogg calls a Pearl Habit.6
  4. After seven days, notice which recipe stuck, and scale it up a little.
