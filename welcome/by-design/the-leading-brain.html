The big idea

Find your sweet spot

You do your best work at a moderate level of arousal: too little and you're bored, too much and you panic. This curve goes back to Yerkes and Dodson in 1908. The authors show how to tune the task, the setting or the stakes so you stay in the zone where focus, and sometimes flow, come naturally.3,4,5,10

Three lessons

1

Name the feeling

Labelling an emotion helps restore control. Suppressing it competes with your thinking.3,7,9

2

Habits slip in small

A large share of daily behavior runs on habit. Small, low-threat steps help new habits form without resistance.4,8

3

Mix your team

Drawing on Helen Fisher's research, the authors describe four temperaments. Strong teams combine them.4,11

The framework

Peak · Change · Teams1,6

The book's three parts, each with three chapters.6

1

Reach your peak

Sweet spot, emotions, focus

Performance depends on a balance of brain chemicals the authors call a neurococktail.2,12

Try: Use STOP: Stop, Take a breath, Observe, Proceed.4
2

Change your brain

Habits, intuition, learning

Build habits in small steps, and give weight to intuition in complex decisions.4,8

Try: Write down a goal that matters to you emotionally.4
3

Build dream teams

Diversity, trust, the future

Explorer, Builder, Director and Negotiator temperaments, plus trust that keeps people in a reward state rather than a threat state.3,4,11

Try: Social pain registers much like physical pain.3,8

From the book

Tools to try

The sweet-spot audit

Based on the book's approach to peak performance.4

  1. List your typical week's activities.
  2. Tag each: bored, stressed, or top of your game.
  3. Turn bored tasks up with challenge or a deadline.
  4. Turn stressful tasks down by breaking them up or getting support.

STOP

A short reset the authors recommend.4

  1. Stop what you're doing.
  2. Take a breath.
  3. Observe your thoughts, feelings and body.
  4. Proceed, on purpose.

Make it yours

Try it this week

  1. Rate today's main task from 1 (bored) to 10 (overwhelmed).
  2. Under 4, add a challenge. Over 7, break it into steps.
  3. Use STOP before one stressful moment each day.
  4. Each night, write three things you're grateful for, and protect your sleep.
