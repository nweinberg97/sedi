The big idea

Feeling good comes first

Abdaal argues the discipline-and-pressure approach has it backwards. Positive feelings give us energy, energy makes work easier to start and keep going, and progress then feels good too. So instead of forcing yourself to work, change how the work feels.1,2,3

Three lessons

1

Make it fun

Ask: what would this look like if it were fun? Small changes, like music, a game, or doing it with someone, can transform a dull task.3

2

Goals that energise

Abdaal suggests NICE goals: Near-term, Input-based, Controllable and Energising, instead of distant targets you can't control.2,4

3

Three kinds of burnout

Overexertion (doing too much), depletion (not recharging) and misalignment (doing what doesn't fit you). Each needs a different fix.2,3

From the book

Tools to try

Taming fear

Fear tools from the Unblock chapters, each borrowed and credited by Abdaal.7

  1. Name the feeling in a word.
  2. Ask how much it will matter in 10 minutes, 10 months and 10 years (Suzy Welch's 10/10/10).8
  3. Remember the spotlight effect: others notice us far less than we think.10

The Energy Investment Portfolio

Abdaal's way to avoid overexertion.5,11

  1. List every commitment and dream competing for your energy.
  2. Choose about five active investments.
  3. Move the rest to a someday list.
  4. Review the portfolio each season.

Make it yours

Try it this week

  1. Pick the task you've been avoiding most.
  2. Change one thing to make it more fun.
  3. Rewrite the goal as a NICE goal.
  4. Set a five-minute timer and start.
